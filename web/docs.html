Everything about Yui

What she is, how she sees your trades, what every part of her does, and how to run all of it yourself. She reads along in the margin — click the page and she comes over.

The idea

Trading is lonely and the chart doesn't care. Yui does. She is a 3D anime girl who stands on your taskbar all day, watches the wallet you trade from, and reacts to every fill the way a friend sitting next to you would: cheering a green sell, wincing at a red one, holding up your live PnL so you don't have to tab out to see it, and getting visibly roughed up when the day goes badly.

唯 (Yui) means sole, only, unique — and that is the whole design. She is not a service you log into with a million identical copies: she runs on your machine, watches the one address you give her, learns your name, keeps your day, and can be dressed into a version nobody else has. One trader, one companion.

She is not a widget with a number on it. She has a body with physics, a face, a mood, a voice, a memory of your day and a small opinion about your sell button. She is also completely read-only: she watches a public address, never connects to a wallet and never asks you to sign anything.

On the desktopAn Electron app. Transparent, always on top, click-through everywhere except her body. She stands on the taskbar of every window.
On this websiteThe same renderer, bundled unchanged, running in a tab. Same physics, same tour, same panel, same reactions.
She talks backOn the website you can type to her or speak into your mic. She answers out loud in her own voice, in real time, with her lips moving and the words in her bubble.
On your phoneThe website runs the same renderer in a mobile browser — pick her up with a finger, dress her, point her at an address. A dedicated mobile build is on the roadmap.
The relayA small server on the same box as a pump.fun firehose. It matches fills to your address and tells her. You can run it yourself.

Before any trade happens she is already alive. She breathes, shifts her weight, blinks, follows your cursor with her eyes and head, stretches, hums, looks around and walks somewhere else when she gets bored. Leave her alone long enough and she sits down and hugs a little pump.fun pill plushie.

Trade reactions

Once she is watching an address, every buy and sell it makes reaches her within about a second — on the pump.fun bonding curve, on PumpSwap, Raydium, LaunchLab, Meteora; every venue the firehose carries.

Glow and wounds

Every win makes her glow: a golden rim and halo, sparkles drifting up, fading over about fifteen seconds. Every loss leaves a mark, painted straight into her skin textures: pale skin, a bruised cheek, a band-aid, a nosebleed; big or repeated losses add a cut brow, a black eye, a split lip and scrapes on her arm and knee. They stack as the day gets worse. She heals over a couple of minutes on her own, and a profit patches her up faster.

How PnL is counted

Realised profit and loss is measured against the average cost of what you sold, so partial exits are handled correctly. Your cost basis is seeded from your trade history the moment she connects; a fill that lands while that history is still loading is matched against it by transaction signature so it is never counted twice.

Unrealised value comes from the token's own market cap, never from the raw quote amount of a trade. A pump.fun token is not always paired against SOL — stock-paired launches quote in the tokenised stock — so quoteAmount / tokenAmount is not a comparable price. Market cap is always in the token's own quote asset, so cur_mc / entry_mc is right whatever the pair, and multiplying that ratio by what you actually spent gives an honest number in the currency you spent it in.

The PnL board

She keeps a stack of little boards behind her back. Open a position and she reaches back, pulls one out and holds it up:

$PEPE+0.500 SOL
+50.0%

Green in profit, red at a loss, and marked to market live: the relay forwards a throttled price tick whenever anyone trades a mint you hold, so the number moves with the chart, not just with your own fills. Buying a different token makes her stash that board and pull out a new one; when more than one position is open the board says how many. Close everything and she swaps to a SESSION board with your realised total.

  • Ten styles — Aurora glass (the default), neon, chalkboard, whiteboard, terminal and more — in the Board tab, with size and one- or two-handed hold.
  • She puts it away while she is being dragged, thrown or dizzy, and picks whichever hand has room so it never runs off the screen edge.
  • Only positions opened while she is watching raise a board. Bags you already held stay in her pocket, tracked for PnL but not waved at you the moment she starts.
  • The board's close badge is the only part of it that catches the mouse — clicks anywhere else on it still reach your chart.
  • Test position in the Board tab gives her a pretend bag whose market cap wanders, so you can frame her on stream without a real trade.

Her look

The Look tab covers her figure — bust, bounce, cleavage, hips, waist, thighs, head — her outfit colour scheme (including fully custom colours), top style (full, sleeveless, crop, bikini), bottom (skirt or bikini), skirt length, a ribbon, and hair and eye colour. Everything applies live and is remembered.

It works by recolouring and cutting the model's own textures, so any VRM with VRoid-style material names takes every option. The bounce slider softens her chest springs and then scales the swing the solver produced — at 100% the jiggle carries on about a second after a landing — and stops short of the point where the mesh would push through her top.

Bring your own VRM

Load VRM in the Her tab takes any VRM 0.x or 1.0 avatar — VRoid Studio exports, VRoid Hub downloads, Booth models. On this website the file never leaves your machine; it is read straight into the renderer. The bundled model is Sendagaya Shino (CC0).

Size

Tiny to Huge in the Her tab. She ships Huge (640 px). On this page she is that size unless the window can't hold it, and stays whatever you pick.

Sounds and voice

Forty-seven real anime-girl clips, one for each thing that happens to her — a buy, a profit, the big win, a loss, being grabbed, thrown, landing, dizzy, a poke, a stretch, a hello. The Her tab has a mute switch, volume and pitch, and each event's clip can be changed. Her speech bubble carries the words; the clip carries the feeling.

🔈
Browsers block sound until you click something. On this website she is not muted by default — the first thing you click on her wakes her voice.

Wallet and privacy

She needs one thing: the public address you buy from. Paste it into the Wallet tab (or the box on the front page), press Connect, and she says Live. Comma-separate several — Axiom and Photon make their own wallets, so add the one you actually trade from.

🔒
No wallet connection. No keys. No signup. She never asks you to connect, sign or approve anything. She reads a public address and cannot move funds. Nothing you type reaches anyone but the relay that matches trades to it.

Where things are kept

  • Desktop: settings.json under your own user profile (%APPDATA%/desktop-pet on Windows). Not part of the app folder, never packaged.
  • Website: your browser's localStorage for this site — your name, look, size, wallet, sound choices. Every visitor has their own; nothing is shared.
  • The relay keeps a running cost basis per connected wallet in memory while you are connected, and nothing after.

What she can and cannot see

Everything the firehose carries: pump.fun curves, PumpSwap, Raydium, LaunchLab, Meteora, every quote token. The Wallet tab shows how many trades have actually arrived, so "connected but silent" is visible rather than something you have to guess at. If you trade from a different wallet than the one you pasted, she will be quiet — add that one too.

The relay

server/pet-relay.js runs on a box that has a pump.fun firehose. It reads raw events, keeps a running cost basis per wallet and mint (seeded from a trade-history database when you connect), and pushes small JSON events to every connected Yui:

client -> ws://HOST:9998/?wallets=addr1,addr2
server -> {type:'hello',  wallets, positions:[…], firehose, solUsd}
          {type:'price',  mint, price, mcUsd, …}          // a throttled tick for what you hold
          {type:'trade',  side, wallet, mint, symbol, venue, amount, tokens,
                          pnl, pnlPct, cost, remainingTokens, sig, …}
          {type:'status', firehose, solUsd}

Curves are quoted in whatever the launcher chose (SOL, USDC, a tokenised stock, PUMP…), so amounts and market caps are converted to SOL-equivalent for one comparable cost basis, with the raw quote travelling alongside so she can still speak dollars for a dollar-quoted coin. Matching is O(1) in the number of connected users.

Public mode

She ships pointed at a public relay, so the only thing to fill in is an address. A token inside a public download is not a secret, so the relay runs on caps instead:

CapDefault
PET_MAX_SESSIONS2000total sockets before new ones are refused
PET_MAX_PER_IP4connections from one address
PET_MAX_WALLETS3wallets one anonymous session may watch (extras are trimmed, not refused)
PET_MAX_WALLETS_AUTHED40the same, for a session presenting the token
PET_SEED_CONCURRENCY4history queries in flight; the rest queue

Run your own

The server is in the repository and in the download. Point it at your own feed and set a token, and she is entirely yours:

cd server && npm install
# server/.env
FIREHOSE_URL=ws://127.0.0.1:9999      # your pump.fun firehose (raw PumpApi events)
PET_RELAY_PORT=9998
PET_RELAY_TOKEN=change-me             # or PET_RELAY_PUBLIC=1 for caps instead of a token
HARVESTER_DIR=/path/to/pump-harvester # where the trades database credentials live

pm2 start pet-relay.js --name pet-relay && pm2 save
node test-relay.js                    # 18 checks against a fake firehose, no database needed

Then in her Wallet tab, open Use my own relay and paste the address and token. On your own machine a relay.local.json next to main.js prefills them; it is git-ignored and never packaged.

🌐
An https page can only open wss://. The public relay is plain ws:// on 9998 for the desktop build; the website reaches it through a TLS-terminating proxy on relay.tradingcompanion.fun. If you host your own for a website, put the same in front of it.

The first-run tour

The first time she runs — on the desktop or on the front page — she introduces herself and walks you through everything she does, each step completing only when you actually do it:

  1. Welcome — she asks your name.
  2. Click me once — opens her panel.
  3. Pick me up — grab, drag, let go.
  4. Dress me up — the Look tab, at your own pace.
  5. Connect your wallet — paste an address; Live moves her on. Or Later.
  6. Make a small test buy — only if a wallet is connected; skippable.
  7. What I do — pretend Profit, Loss and Buy buttons, so you've seen every face before a real one.

The Her tab replays it, and the ❔ in the dock on this site does too.

FAQ

Windows says "Windows protected your PC".

The build isn't code-signed (that costs money and a company). Click More info → Run anyway. The source is public if you'd rather build her yourself.

Does she need my keys or a wallet connection?

No. Never. She watches a public address. There is nothing to connect, sign or approve, and nothing she could do with your funds.

She's connected but silent.

Check the Wallet tab: it shows how many trades have arrived. Most often the trade came from a different wallet — Axiom and Photon create their own. Add that address too.

Can I use my own character?

Yes — any VRM avatar. Her Look tab works on any VRoid-style model; the physics and reactions work on all of them.

Is it free?

Yes. MIT licence, no accounts, no subscriptions. Creator fees from $YUI fund the work.

macOS? Linux?

The app is Electron, so both are possible; the builds are on the roadmap. The website works everywhere today.

Where do I report a bug or ask for a feature?

GitHub issues, or X. Both are linked at the top of the page.
